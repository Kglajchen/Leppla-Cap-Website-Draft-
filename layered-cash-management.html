The structure

Built Around When Cash Is Needed

Once the cash-flow review is done, the first question is not “what should we buy?” It is “what must stay available, and when?” Layered Cash Management answers that question. It is built for surplus operating cash that still has a job: payroll, dues, benefits, grants, payables, and the reserve that keeps the organization steady.

Philosophy

Cash that pays bills is not an investment theme. It is a scheduling problem. We keep your bank as the front door, then place surplus cash into duration sleeves so money is ready when it is needed. As dates approach, liquidity flows downhill toward the bank. We do not chase yield by locking operating capital into products you cannot exit.

How We Build It

From the cash-flow map we set an insured operating float at your bank, then schedule the rest across near-term, quarterly, annual, and optional second-year sleeves. Holdings are primarily U.S. Treasuries timed to that calendar. The fee is 25 basis points, locked for two years.

What This Is Not

It is not a substitute for your checking relationship. It is not a long-horizon equity mandate. And it is not a place for illiquid, high-fee products that look attractive on a slide and fail when cash is actually required.

How it works

Layered Treasury Bills That Move Toward Maturity

Layered Cash Management is a ladder of U.S. Treasury bills sized to your cash-flow calendar. Each layer holds bills timed to when that cash is expected to be needed. Your bank (L0) stays outside this account. The managed layers begin at L1.

The waterfall
As bills age toward maturity, they waterfall down the stack: L4 becomes L3, L3 becomes L2, L2 becomes L1. Liquidity flows downhill toward the dates when cash is actually needed, and ultimately toward your bank.
Reinvest at the top
When an L1 bill matures, cash that is not needed at the bank is reinvested at the top into a new longer sleeve. That keeps the ladder full: near-term layers stay stocked, and longer layers keep earning until they roll down.
Why Treasuries
Treasury bills are liquid, scheduled, and fit a duration ladder. The point is availability on known dates for money that still has an operating job.

The Layers

L1 Through L4, Read From The Bottom Up

  1. L4

    Two Year

    Optional second-year sleeve. New purchases and reinvestment usually enter here (or at the longest sleeve in use), then waterfall down unless cash is needed sooner at the bank.

  2. L3

    Next Year

    The twelve-month sleeve. Bills here waterfall toward L2 and L1 as the calendar advances, keeping a full year of scheduled liquidity in motion.

  3. L2

    Next Quarter

    Bills sized to the next several months. As they approach maturity, they waterfall into L1. Typical for insurance renewals, seasonal costs, and planned payables.

  4. L1

    Next Month

    Bills maturing in the coming weeks. Closest to the bank. When they mature, cash can move to your operating account for near-term bills, payroll, and dues, or be sent back to the top of the ladder.
