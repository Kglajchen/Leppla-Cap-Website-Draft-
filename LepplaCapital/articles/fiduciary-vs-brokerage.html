Fiduciary duty

Fiduciary vs brokerage

The difference is not cosmetic. It shows up in what gets sold, what can be exited, and whose interest comes first.

5 min readEducational

A Registered Investment Adviser acting as a fiduciary is obligated to put client interests first. A brokerage relationship can center on selling products. Both may use professional language. They are not the same job.

What goes wrong in a product sale

Funds can end up in holdings that are hard to understand and hard to exit: illiquid, high-fee, long-duration products that do not support operating cash needs. When sale is attempted, there may be no real market. That is not a theoretical risk. It is how Leppla Capital’s founding work began.

What a fiduciary review asks

Is it suitable? Does the holding match liquidity needs and mandate?

Is it understandable? Can the board explain what they own?

Is the economics clear? Fees, commissions, and opportunity cost in the open.

A fiduciary conversation starts with the mandate and the cash. A product conversation often starts with the inventory.

A practical standard

For operating cash: keep the bank as the front door and schedule surplus by duration. For longer reserves: use liquid building blocks and a written risk framework. Publish the fee. Prefer clarity over complexity.
