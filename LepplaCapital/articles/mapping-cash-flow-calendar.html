Cash flow

How to map your cash-flow calendar

Before you invest surplus cash, write down when money comes in and when it must go out. That calendar is the foundation of Layered Cash Management.

5 min readEducational

Most organizations already know their big bills. Payroll, rent, insurance, benefit remittances, grants, and seasonal spikes are familiar. What is often missing is a single written calendar that turns that knowledge into clear cash buckets.

Start with the last 12 to 24 months

Pull bank activity if you can. Mark the low points, not only the averages. Low points tell you how much must stay ready for operations when timing is tight.

List what you know is coming

Add known obligations for the next year: contracts, insurance renewals, conferences, capital projects, and any contingency the board expects you to keep available.

Separate three questions

1. What must be ready now? Immediate bills and surprises.

2. What is needed within the next quarter? Near-term, but not tomorrow.

3. What can wait longer? Planned needs at 6 to 24 months, or true multi-year surplus.

If a balance has sat above your operating floor for months with no planned use, it may be idle cash. That does not mean it should take market risk. It means it deserves a plan.

Why this comes first

Duration decides the layer. A dollar needed next month cannot sit in a longer sleeve just because yields look attractive. Mapping the calendar first keeps the conversation honest and keeps your bank as the front door for operating cash.
