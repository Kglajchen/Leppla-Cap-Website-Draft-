Risk tolerance

Understanding your organization's risk profile

The right portfolio begins with liquidity needs and risk tolerance. Those two factors shape how we use the Securities Investment Pyramid™.

InstitutionsEducational

Your investment strategy should reflect the cash-flow needs and risk tolerance of your organization. At Leppla Capital, we treat those as the starting point for longer-horizon reserves, not an afterthought.

Two questions that matter

Risk tolerance

How does the organization feel about investment risk and swings in portfolio value?

  • Low: Prefers stability and is uncomfortable with significant swings, even if that means lower returns.
  • Moderate: Can accept some volatility in exchange for higher long-term growth potential.
  • High: Comfortable with substantial fluctuations because the time horizon is long and growth is the priority.

Cash flow / distribution needs

When and how much does the organization expect to distribute from the portfolio?

  • Near-term (1–5 years): Significant distributions required soon. Capital preservation and predictable income take priority.
  • Intermediate (5–10 years): Moderate distribution needs over the medium term. Some volatility can be tolerated for growth.
  • Long-term (10+ years): Limited near-term distributions. Greater flexibility to accept volatility for higher long-term returns.

There are no right or wrong answers. The goal is to find the profile that fits the spending policy and the organization's comfort with volatility.

How Leppla Capital uses these factors

We use seven model profiles that combine cash-flow needs and risk tolerance. Each profile has a clear philosophy and an allocation framework built with the Securities Investment Pyramid™. Broader pyramid layers are added only as the written mandate supports them.

Seven institutional profiles

1. Very Conservative — Near-term needs, very low risk tolerance. Focus on principal protection, short-term fixed income, cash equivalents, and stable value.

2. Conservative — Near-term needs with some flexibility, low risk tolerance. High-quality fixed income with a small allocation to broad equity market beta.

3. Moderately Conservative — 1–5 year needs with moderate flexibility. Balanced mix of fixed income and diversified equities, with emphasis on lower-volatility holdings.

4. Balanced — Roughly 5–7 year needs, moderate risk tolerance. Core diversified equities plus investment-grade fixed income, with moderate sector exposure.

5. Moderately Aggressive — 5–12 year growth orientation. Growth-oriented equities with meaningful sector and thematic exposure.

6. Aggressive — 10–20 year horizon, high risk tolerance. Heavy equity allocation with sector, thematic, and some direct investment opportunities.

7. Very Aggressive — 15–35 year maximum growth focus, very high risk tolerance. Maximum equity growth with substantial thematic, sector, and direct exposure.

Pyramid layer definitions

Foundational: The base of the portfolio. Broad diversification, higher capital preservation, and a stable core. Principal protection is the primary objective.

Fixed Income: High-quality fixed income, primarily U.S. Treasuries, corporate bonds, and municipal bonds (AA–AAA / Aa3–Aaa), for principal protection, liquidity, and modest income.

Market-Beta: Market-matching passive mutual funds or ETFs (for example, broad S&P 500 exposure).

Alpha: Excess return sought through active decisions above passive market returns. In the pyramid, the upper layers collectively represent the alpha-seeking portion.

Asset Class: Allocations across major equity categories such as large-cap growth, large-cap value, small cap, international, and emerging markets.

Sector: Targeted exposure to specific market sectors through sector-focused ETFs.

Thematic: Allocations to long-term structural trends, such as artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, or clean energy.

Direct: Investments made directly into a particular security, rather than through a pooled fund.

Mutual funds and ETFs are pooled vehicles that hold baskets of securities. Mutual funds are typically priced at end of day on net asset value. ETFs trade throughout the day.
