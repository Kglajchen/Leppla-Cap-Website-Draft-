Fees

Fixed fees in plain English

Published fees and a two-year lock are not marketing slogans. They change how much of the return stays with the client.

4 min readEducational

Many advisory relationships quote a fee that sounds small as a percentage. Over time, that percentage compounds against the account. A lower, published fee leaves more of the gross return with the organization or individual that owns the capital.

What “fixed” and “locked” mean here

Leppla Capital publishes its fees: 25 basis points for Layered Cash Management and 50 basis points for the Securities Investment Pyramid™. The fee is locked for two years. Every two years it restructures to the same stated basis points of the then-current account value for the next two-year period.

Why the lock matters

A lock is about certainty. The client knows the rate for the engagement term. The firm does not quietly reprice midstream. At renewal, the rate resets to the published basis points against the then-current value.

Fee drag is not theoretical. On the same assumed return, a higher ongoing fee can leave a material gap over a decade. See How We Invest for a plain illustration.

What to ask any adviser

1. What is the all-in fee, in basis points?

2. Is it published and locked, or discretionary?

3. What else is paid in commissions, spreads, or product costs?
