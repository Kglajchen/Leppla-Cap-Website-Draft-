The structure

Built From The Foundation Up

The Securities Investment Pyramid™ is a distinct account for longer-horizon reserves. It does not replace your bank. It sits beside Layered Cash Management when the cash-flow review shows true surplus beyond operating needs.

Philosophy

Longer reserves deserve a framework, not a product pile. We build from the foundation up: stability first, then market exposure, then more selective risk only where the mandate and written approval allow. The goal is disciplined participation in markets without confusing reserve capital for operating cash.

How We Build It

The cash-flow review tells us what is truly surplus. That surplus is placed in a distinct account and constructed in layers by risk tolerance. Foundational and fixed income holdings come first. Market-beta and higher layers are added only as the written policy supports them. The fee is 50 basis points, locked for two years.

What This Is Not

It is not a brokerage product shelf. It is not a replacement for payroll or checking. And it is not permission to stretch into complex, illiquid holdings because a presentation made them look sophisticated.

How it works

A Pyramid Of Layers Governed By Risk Tolerance

The Securities Investment Pyramid™ organizes longer-horizon capital from a stable base upward. Each layer adds a different kind of market exposure. How far up the pyramid you go depends on the risk profile and the written mandate.

Foundation first
Portfolios begin with Foundational and Fixed Income holdings. Stability and liquidity come before selective risk. Read the graphic from the bottom up.
Broader layers with approval
Market-Beta, Alpha, Asset Class, Sector, Thematic, and Direct layers are added only as risk tolerance and written approval support them. Higher on the pyramid means more selective exposure.
Separate from operating cash
This account is for capital that can stay invested. Near-term bills and operating float belong at the bank and in Layered Cash Management.

The Layers

Foundational Through Direct, Read From The Bottom Up

  1. 08

    Direct

    Investments made directly into a particular security rather than through a pooled fund. Used only with explicit written approval.

  2. 07

    Thematic

    Allocations to long-term structural trends such as artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, or clean energy, when the profile supports them.

  3. 06

    Sector

    Targeted exposure to specific market sectors, typically through sector-focused funds or ETFs, within the bounds of the written mandate.

  4. 05

    Asset Class

    Allocations across major equity categories such as large-cap growth and value, small cap, international, and emerging markets.

  5. 04

    Alpha

    Active decisions aimed at excess return above passive market returns. Upper layers collectively represent the more selective, alpha-seeking portion.

  6. 03

    Market-Beta

    Broad market exposure through passive funds or ETFs that track major indexes. The first step into equity market participation for many profiles.

  7. 02

    Fixed Income

    High-quality fixed income for principal protection, liquidity, and modest income. Typically U.S. Treasuries and high-grade corporate or municipal bonds.

  8. 01

    Foundational

    The base of the portfolio. Broad diversification, higher capital preservation, and a stable core. Principal protection is the primary objective.
